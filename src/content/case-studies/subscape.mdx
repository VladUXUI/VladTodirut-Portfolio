<SectionTitle eyebrow="The idea & challenge">Make governance fun and engaging.</SectionTitle>

Subscape is a sub-brand of Taho Wallet.

DAO governance is usually boring. Voting dashboards, endless proposals, and a handful of whales making all the decisions. Not exactly engaging.

With Subscape, we wanted to make governance feel alive. What if participating in the DAO felt more like playing a strategy game than filling out a ballot?

The hard part was obvious: how do you gamify governance without making it feel like a gimmick?

- **Complex mechanics scare people away.** Most folks don't want to learn a whole new game just to stake tokens.
- **Governance usually feels thankless.** You vote, you click, nothing changes for you personally.
- **Engagement is fragile.** A shiny new system can spike participation at first, but if it's shallow, people churn fast.

So the challenge was: design a system that feels playful but still meaningful, with real stakes and a reason to keep coming back.

---

<SectionTitle eyebrow="The design">Feel part of something.</SectionTitle>

Subscape is a **map-control game.**

- Users join one of several **Realms**. Each Realm is a subDAO with its own identity and members.
- By completing **quests** and staking TAHO, users earn XP. The more active the Realm, the bigger its share of token emissions.
- **XP** isn't just a number. It's the basis for rewards, status, and influence inside the Realm.

The design leaned into light strategy mechanics: map control, community identity, and friendly competition. Enough to make things interesting without overloading users with complexity.

<SectionTitle eyebrow="Results">How did it go?</SectionTitle>

We started with 5 Realms (Arbitrum, Frax, Gitcoin, CyberConnect, zkSync), and quickly grew to 7 with Base and Galxe joining us after the beta launch.

The Beta ran for 6 weeks, with 60k users joining a Realm and competing to win the most XP.
