<SectionTitle eyebrow="Intro">Your music, your persona</SectionTitle>

A client came to me with a new product idea. In music right now, it's really hard to prove you're actually a fan, let alone get rewarded for it. VIP tickets and exclusive drops get scooped up by bots and resellers, and the people who really care about the music get left behind.

Their idea: what if listening itself could be the proof? They needed a prototype to pitch Echoes, and to get their core team on the same page about what it would be.

I was the only designer. The client brought the problem, and I came up with the product around it: the persona, the points system, the onboarding and the clay look.

<SectionTitle eyebrow="Decision #1">A persona that grows with your taste</SectionTitle>

Listening history is invisible, and "proof of fandom" is pretty abstract. I wanted something people could actually see, feel is theirs, and want to grow.

So onboarding gives you a starter persona. It's very basic at first, just a colour and a letter. Connect Spotify and it levels up straight away, and you can see your taste start to shape it.

<Figure src={personaBusts} alt="Clay persona busts for different music tastes" narrow caption="Different tastes, different personas." />

<SectionTitle eyebrow="Decision #2">Points that work like XP</SectionTitle>

Points in a rewards app usually work like money: save them up, spend them on a cheaper ticket. That's not what we wanted here.

In Echoes, bands and artists reward real fans based on their XP. So points are more like a reputation score than a currency, and that's why they look like XP (a circle with a lightning bolt) instead of coins. If you could just spend them, you'd end up with the same buying and reselling the product is trying to get away from.

<SectionTitle eyebrow="Decision #4">Why clay</SectionTitle>

I tried a few styles before landing on clay. The glossy, glass-like 3D looked nice but didn't feel personal. The flat confetti, cartoon headphones and bright clay notes felt a bit too childish.

Textured clay felt more personal and more human. People connect clay with two stages of life. As kids it's playful and messy, and as adults it turns into pottery and art, something you make and are proud of. That fit the idea of a persona you build over time really well.

<Figure src={vibeExplorations} alt="Illustration style explorations leading to the clay aesthetic" narrow rounded={false} caption="Not personal enough on the left, a bit too childish in the middle, clay on the right." />

I also ran the main screens through an AI attention tool to check people would look where I wanted them to. The intro and perks screens scored 94 and 97 for focus. The email step only got 67, so that's the first screen I'd go back to.

<SectionTitle eyebrow="Reflection">Looking back</SectionTitle>

The most fun part was using AI to create the clay personas. Instead of drawing a fixed set of avatars, I could play with a persona that changes as your music taste changes, and that made the whole idea much easier to get in a pitch.

The big question a prototype can't answer is whether fans actually care about being recognised for their XP, and whether artists would use it. That's the first thing I'd want to test with real people.
